What is Fair Fight?
In Torn, Fair Fight (FF) is a multiplier on how hard a hit was relative to the attacker. Higher FF means the defender was closer to the attacker in battle strength, so you earn more base respect on chains and better scoring on many war hits. Lower FF means you attacked someone much weaker than you, so respect and some war scoring are discounted.
Torn documents the concept on the
wiki (Fair fights).
After each hit, the faction chain log and attack history show the FF value Torn calculated for that attack.

Battle Stat Score (BSS)
Before Fair Fight, Torn reduces your four battle stats to a single number called Battle Stat Score (BSS). FFScouter uses the same definition as Torn’s battle-stat API: the sum of the square root of each stat, rounded to a whole number.

Worked example: calculate FF from two BSS values
You attack someone with BSS 4,000. Their BSS is 2,000.
Ratio = 2,000 ÷ 4,000 = 0.5
FF = 1 + (8 ÷ 3) × 0.5 = 1 + 1.333… ≈ 2.33
Worked example: estimate defender BSS from a hit
You know your own BSS (4,000) and Torn reported FF 2.50 on the attack. Rearrange:
BSSdefender = (3 ÷ 8) × (2.50 − 1) × 4,000 = 0.375 × 1.5 × 4,000 = 2,250
That is exactly how FFScouter learns opponents’ public BSS from volunteer attack logs when you are the attacker.
How FFScouter uses this
When you register with an API key, FFScouter pulls your battle stats and attack history. Your stats become bss_private (this isn't shared with anyone else). Each qualifying attack includes a Fair Fight value; combined with your BSS, FFScouter estimates each opponent’s bss_public and stores it in a shared pool other players can query on Player View, Target Finder, and the JSON API.
To improve the pool, FFScouter only learns from attacks where Torn’s reported FF is strictly between about 1.05 and 3.00 (hits showing exactly 1.00 or 3.00 are skipped), the attack is recent enough, finished cleanly, and not interrupted. Those rules mirror Torn’s display cap and keep bad data out of the estimator.
For anyone else looking you up, FFScouter computes calculated FF from your public BSS and theirs using the formula above. That value can exceed 3.00 when the true gap is huge, even though Torn’s chain UI often caps at 3.00.

Wars and chains
Ranked wars
During a war, most factions want hits at FF 2 or higher when possible so each attack contributes more war score. Farming far below your range steals points from teammates who need those targets. Your leadership may set stricter rules; use FFScouter to stay in range before you commit energy.
Chains and events
On chains, higher Fair Fight raises base respect per hit. During chaining events (for example Halloween), keeping a high average FF across the chain can dramatically increase respect for the same number of hits compared with farming weak targets.
During busy events, use Target Finder with Not in Hosp List to pick up targets who are out of hospital and in your FF range. Pair it with your faction’s chain discipline.

What FF and BSS do not include
Fair Fight and BSS are based only on the four battle stats through the square-root sum. They do not reflect temporary or situational power, including:
Grenades, weapons, and armor you equip for one fight
Books, education, and other long-term upgrades not already in your battle stats
Job perks, company specials, and faction bonuses
Drugs, blood bags, medical cooldowns, or other combat consumables
Two players with the same BSS can still feel very different in a live fight. Use FF as a range guide, not a guarantee of outcome.
Limits and premium
The FF-based public model works well for the vast majority of players, but it has known limits:
Torn caps displayed FF at 3.00, which blocks learning from many hits on very weak or very strong targets.
Extreme stat imbalance (stat whoring) makes BSS a rough proxy for true total stats.
Some top battle-stat players always report FF 3.00 in logs, so the reverse formula rarely applies to them.
Generally, the math breaks down above about 20 billion total battle stats, where gaps are huge and public FF alone cannot pin down opponents reliably.
FFScouter Premium adds other data sources so estimates stay useful where Fair Fight alone hits a ceiling, as well as providing other tools like the flight tracker, stat distribution breakdowns and faction tools. Basic public estimates remain free for everyone.
